Decision-alts — text-only treatments

Each option uses real Decision 2 (Vocabulary) content from Feel Check — a non-visual decision with no thumbnails to add. Same body prose before each treatment, same continuation after, so we can judge how naturally each handles a pure-text alts case.

Director's recommendation

Option 1 — Inline prose

Universal. Works for visual AND logic decisions equally. No chrome. The alternatives ARE the prose — there's no separate component to interrupt the flow, no abrupt table to fix. Reads as a confident designer voice: "I considered X, Y, landed on Z." Senior portfolios trust prose to carry rigor; junior portfolios need explicit callouts.

The other 3 text treatments (3, 4, 5) all work — they're just different flavors of "make the alternatives a discrete component." Option 1 says: don't make it a component at all.

Option 1 · Recommended

Inline prose

Alternatives folded into the body paragraph. Zero chrome. No separate component. The rigor signal comes from how the prose is written — "I tested three options. X failed because Y. Z worked because W."

The spec used clinical labels — "Sparkling," "Affirmation," "Set Intention." In the in-house tutor test, students hesitated, asked what they meant, or skipped these options entirely. The flow worked; the vocabulary didn't.

I tested three vocabulary approaches. Keeping the spec's clinical labels would have required tutor explanations every session. Direct synonyms ("Sparkling" → "Joyful") still felt adult — they didn't fix the hesitation. I landed on child-natural rewrites: "Sparkling" became "Excited," "Set Intention" became "I'm ready." I took the final wording back to Roots for clinical sign-off before the external rollout.
