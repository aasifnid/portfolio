Cuemath Partners

Partners promoting Cuemath's MathFest events had no place to see their work — every referral, registration, enrollment, and payout was tracked manually by Cuemath's business ops team. I designed a three-part system: a sign-up landing page, a mobile dashboard where partners check it all in real time, and an admin tool that replaced the manual workflow.

01

The Problem

Partners drove sign-ups through their referral links — but tracked progress in their head, and every payout mismatch turned into a ticket and a wait.

After every payout, the partner compared their count against what landed in their account. When the two didn't match — and they often didn't — the partner had no way to find out why on their own. The only fix: a ticket to ops, and a wait that could span days.

This worked when the partner community was small. As Cuemath scaled event-driven acquisition across the USA, the opacity that frustrated a handful of partners would only multiply as the community grew.

02

Role

I owned design for Cuemath Partners end-to-end — Cuemath's community-driven distribution programme for US MathFest events, built across three surfaces from sign-up to admin.

  • End-to-end UX/UI — sign-up landing page (web), partner dashboard (mobile), admin dashboard (web)
  • Partner journey design — sign-up → referral tracking → real-time payout status, all in one system
  • Trust-through-transparency — couldn't pay partners more to win trust; built it instead through earnings clarity and real-time payout status
  • Cross-functional collaboration — PM (scope), FE intern + BE engineer (build), business ops (payout structure)
03

Decisions

Three decisions shaped the partner experience: how trust got built before partners signed up, how partners saw their own work afterward, and how the system kept their two types of rewards clear.

Decision 1 — Trust as the spine: borrowed Cuemath's credibility, didn't build it from scratch

Cuemath already had two things partners would trust before signing up: a 4.8+ Trustpilot rating across 80+ countries, and a community of 1,000+ existing volunteers. The landing page used those (Trustpilot reviews on the page, community size stated, brand reach named) rather than new claims I'd have to prove. The programme didn't have to build trust from scratch; it inherited it from what Cuemath had already earned.

The bet worked. 138 community educators signed up as partners across the USA in 9 months — onboarded without paid ads or a brand campaign, riding the credibility Cuemath had built before.

Trustpilot reviews, awards and mentions embedded on the landing page — borrowed credibility, not claimed.

Decision 2 — Dashboard as the partner's visibility into their own work

The landing page brings partners in once; the dashboard is where they live afterward. I designed it around the question they used to ask ops manually: "Where are my referrals, and when do I get paid?" Referral progress (registered → trial → enrolled), payout status (assigned → verified → processed → paid), and earnings totals — all visible at a glance. The dashboard replaced the ticket-and-wait cycle with answers partners could check on their own.

Partners came back. 3,019 active users hit the dashboard across 9 months — roughly 22 visits per onboarded partner, peaking in January when the US admissions cycle returned.

The partner dashboard — referral progress, payout status, and earnings totals all visible at a glance.

Decision 3 — Two-track reward system: events and direct referrals as separate flows

Partners did two different kinds of work, and the rewards reflected that. Event registrations paid $2–5 each (small, fast); direct referrals into the tutoring program paid $100–200 each (large, longer wait). The dashboard kept these separate — an Events tab and a Referrals tab, each with its own progress view and payout structure. Partners could see which kind of work was paying them, and how much, without mixing the two up.

The split also showed who was most active. The top partner drove 25 referrals in 9 months — a meaningful concentration of effort. Without the separation, that signal would have been buried.

05

Lessons

Instrument activation from day one — sign-ups aren't enough

We tracked sign-ups, but not whether partners actually shared their link. A signed-up partner who never shares is a dead conversion. Next time: instrument activation from launch (did they share? when? how many times?) and build a nudge flow — "It's been 48 hours since you signed up. Try sharing your link with one person."

Connected surfaces need simultaneous design, not sequential

I designed the landing page, then the partner dashboard, then realized ops needed an admin tool. What an partner sees must match what an admin can manage — these should be designed together. Designing them in sequence created inconsistencies we had to fix after launch.

Trust is shown, not said — and placed before the commitment, not after

The FAQ addressed every payout question in text, but text doesn't build trust as effectively as visual flow. A payout status timeline — "Referral assigned → Verified → Processed → Paid" with actual dates — reduces payout anxiety more than any paragraph of copy. The same principle applies at the moment of commitment: trust signals before the sign-up form outperformed reassurance after. Where the user feels at risk, design needs to show motion — and show it early.
