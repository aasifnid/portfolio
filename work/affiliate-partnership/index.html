Affiliate Partnership

Affiliates promoting Cuemath's MathFest events had no place to track their work — referrals, registrations, enrollments, and payouts were all managed manually by Cuemath's business ops team on a spreadsheet. I designed a three-part system: a sign-up landing page, a mobile dashboard where affiliates check it all in real time, and an admin tool that replaced the spreadsheet for Cuemath.

01

The Problem

Cuemath had to fill 200+ weekend events across the US — and neither ads (₹1L/enrollment) nor a national sales team could scale to that affordably.

Cuemath's US acquisition had been running on performance marketing — Meta and Google ads — but cost per enrollment had climbed to ₹1 lakh and the unit economics broke. The company moved to in-person MathFest events as a cheaper alternative; by 2024 events had become the primary US channel. But events created their own bottleneck.

To fill 200+ events every weekend, you either hire a national sales team — too expensive — or you find people who already have trust and reach with parents in each region. Cuemath's bet: community educators (tutors, education consultants, homeschool leaders) could become local promoters if the incentive was right. The affiliate programme was that bet.

02

Role

I owned the entire product end-to-end — UX, interaction design, and visual assets across all three surfaces: the affiliate sign-up landing page, the affiliate dashboard, and the admin dashboard. No dedicated PM or researcher was assigned; I partnered with engineering for implementation and ops for payout structure decisions.

Success criteria were defined upfront: affiliates must sign up and receive their unique referral link within 5 minutes; the dashboard must give them a clear reason to log in repeatedly; the payout process must be transparent and trustworthy; and all three surfaces had to work as one system.

One hard constraint shaped the work: ops defined a fixed payout budget per event. I couldn't solve "build trust through big payouts." Instead, I had to solve it through transparency and speed — making it crystal clear how much affiliates could earn, and sometimes paying them before the event ended to signal trustworthiness.

How I got to the design

Three modes of inquiry. No formal user research; this was an end-to-end build against a tight timeline.

Method Sample What it told us
Competitive audit 5+ affiliate programs Leading with earnings transparency signals confidence, not transaction. Almost every successful programme does it.
Stakeholder conversations Ops + early affiliates New affiliates default to distrust: "Will I get paid?" Trust signals must come before the form, not after.
Internal landing page A/B Two structures, internal Hero → trust signals → form converted higher than Hero → form → trust footer.
03

Decisions

New affiliates don't trust you. They're thinking: "Will I actually get paid? How much money are we really talking about? Is this a scam or a real opportunity?" Cuemath's instinct was to answer these after the sign-up form. That's backwards — new people don't enter personal details until they trust the system. You need credibility before the commitment.

Decision 1 — Lead with earnings transparency, not brand mission

Landing page hero: "Earn up to $2,000/month promoting MathFest events." FAQ upfront with explicit payout structure: $2–5 per successful registration, $100–200 per direct admission. Volunteers are dual-motivated — impact and income. Naming the income openly signals confidence, not transaction.

Hero framing

Lead with brand mission

"Help children love math." Affiliates can't pay rent with mission. Felt evasive about the deal.

Earnings disclosed only after signup

Introduces distrust at the worst moment, after personal info is in.

Earnings disclosed in hero

Signals confidence; affiliates self-qualify before form.

"Naming the income openly signals confidence, not transaction."

From: Competitive audit of 5+ affiliate programs.

Decision 2 — Trust signals before the form, not after

Trustpilot reviews (4.8+), community size ("1,000+ volunteers"), payout terms, and FAQs all placed before the sign-up form. Affiliates answer "Is this legitimate?" themselves, then proceed.

Layout

Hero → Form → Trust signals in footer

Rejected in internal testing: significantly lower conversion.

Long scroll with form at bottom only

High-intent visitors couldn't shortcut to action.

Hero → Trust signals → Form

Converted significantly higher in two-version test.

"When users are wary, trust signals belong before the commitment, not after."

From: Internal landing-page A/B comparison.

Decision 3 — Dashboard as the engagement surface, not the landing page

The landing page's job is conversion. Whether affiliates keep promoting is determined by the dashboard — referral link, conversion tracking, earnings, payout history, campaign materials. The dashboard had to give them a reason to log in repeatedly.

Engagement surface

Minimal dashboard, email-driven updates

Emails get ignored; passive UX.

Static link + monthly summary

No tight feedback loop on referral behavior.

Live dashboard with all signals

Clear reason to log in: "Check if my referrals are converting."

"The landing page converts once; the dashboard has to earn the next login."

From: Affiliate retention thinking — landing-page vs dashboard division of labor.

Decision 4 — Flexible payout timing as a trust-builder

Payouts normally happen 10 business days after event end — that's a long lag during which affiliates wonder if they'll be paid. Operationally, Cuemath sometimes paid before events ended for affiliates hitting early milestones. We made that visible in the dashboard (assigned → verified → processed → paid, with dates). Affiliates see money move; trust compounds.

The affiliate sign-up landing page — earnings in the hero, trust signals before the form, FAQ open by default.

All three surfaces launched: the public landing page, the affiliate dashboard (referral link, stats, earnings, payout history, campaign materials), and the admin dashboard for Cuemath to manage affiliates and process payouts. The programme went live, and educators began signing up and promoting MathFest events.

05

Lessons

Instrument activation from day one — sign-ups aren't enough

We tracked sign-ups, but not whether affiliates actually shared their link. A signed-up affiliate who never shares is a dead conversion. Next time: instrument activation from launch (did they share? when? how many times?) and build a nudge flow — "It's been 48 hours since you signed up. Try sharing your link with one person."

Connected surfaces need simultaneous design, not sequential

I designed the landing page, then the affiliate dashboard, then realized ops needed an admin tool. What an affiliate sees must match what an admin can manage — these should be designed together. Designing them in sequence created inconsistencies we had to fix after launch.

Trust is shown, not said — and placed before the commitment, not after

The FAQ addressed every payout question in text, but text doesn't build trust as effectively as visual flow. A payout status timeline — "Referral assigned → Verified → Processed → Paid" with actual dates — reduces payout anxiety more than any paragraph of copy. The same principle applies at the moment of commitment: trust signals before the sign-up form outperformed reassurance after. Where the user feels at risk, design needs to show motion — and show it early.
